# Knockout Day against Hitman: a review

This compares Knockout Day with the Hitman World of Assassination trilogy on its core loop: explore, observe, plan, act, adapt. It also borrows from Untitled Goose Game, Thief, Dishonored, RDR2, Watch Dogs 2 and Saints Row where they do one part better. It was written after this round's changes.

## 1. Missions: is there something to do, and do you know it?

**Hitman:**
- You get a target and a briefing.
- Mission Stories ("Opportunities") point you at one setup without solving it.
- Challenges list ideas you haven't tried.
- The level tells you what the goal is. The route is your problem.

**Knockout Day now:**
- The goal is England going through. Every match also has a to-do list, which is Goose Game's list rather than Hitman's: get in, flatten a steward, streak, ring in a bomb threat, shut a grass up, and so on.
- Leads (ways in) are written down when you hear them.
- Hidden stories reward curiosity without announcing themselves: Lonely Len, the stag do, Mad Mick's bounty, Off-duty Dave and a kind stranger.

**Gap:** Nothing joins "England go through" to "here is a way to make it happen". Hitman's Mission Stories are the bridge. Each ground could have three or four "fixes", tracked once heard about:
- dope their keeper;
- get their star arrested;
- bribe the referee;
- stop the match while England are ahead.

Each fix would be a short chain of steps, written as overheard lines and leads. It should never be a waypoint.

## 2. Exploration: is the space worth exploring, and can you read it?

**Hitman:** A level has three or four layers: public, guarded, staff-only and VIP. Each layer has its own disguise, and the way between layers is visible but locked. Landmarks such as the big screen, the fountain and the stage let you navigate without a map.

**Knockout Day:**
- **Inside:** Before this round, the stands were a flat block of seats and the way in wasn't clear. Now each gangway has:
  - a floodlight pool spilling onto the concourse;
  - a painted block panel (S1 to S3, W, E for away) with arrows;
  - an aisle of steps running up through the seats.
- **Outside:** The street is lively and readable.
- **Layers:** Street, then concourse and stands, then staff corridors, then dressing rooms and pitch. That matches Hitman's public, rule-bound, private and back-of-house spaces well.

**Gaps:**
- The concourse is thin, about six metres deep, so the turnstiles, the bar and the stand mouths crowd together. A deeper concourse with the bar on its own wall would help: queues, a big screen showing the match, a programme seller with a pitch.
- There is no landmark visible from far away inside the ground. A big screen or scoreboard at one end would let you orient by eye.
- Restricted areas could show their status before you step in, as Hitman's "Trespassing" warning does under the minimap. The door hint does this for doors but not for floors.

## 3. Observation: can you watch routines and act on them?

**Hitman's strongest pillar.** Guards and targets loop on visible schedules. Instinct shows them through walls. Overheard conversations point you at a moment, such as "the chef goes for a smoke at noon".

**Knockout Day has the parts:**
- the kit man's pub run;
- the steward who nips out for a fag;
- Lenny's smoke;
- the beer lorry;
- the ref's tea at half-time;
- the reporter dropping a press pass;
- Para Steve's jump.

The radar shows stewards' eyelines. Gossip, which is now big-variance with tips weighted in, tells you about these routines.

**Gaps:**
- Routines are hard to watch. Nothing tells you something is about to happen, such as a clock above the kit man ("back in 2 min").
- A Hitman-style "tracked target" option would help: tap someone in the ring, choose "Watch", and their next stop and time appear for a minute. It keeps the planning optional, short and one step deep, which is the line Mimimi draws in Desperados III.

## 4. Planning: can you set things up and make them come together?

**Hitman:** Plans are chains: get the disguise, poison the drink, wait for the toast. Systems interlock:
- a dropped coin draws one guard;
- a body draws many;
- disguises are judged by "enforcers", not by everyone.

**Knockout Day after this round has real interlocking verbs:**
- **Framing.** Plant gear on a player, then ring 999 from a burner. He's arrested at the next stoppage, including on the pitch.
- **Lures.**
  - A walkie decoy ("all units to the bar") sends every steward in range across the ground.
  - A bomb threat holds kick-off or stops the match.
  - Streaking stops play.
- **Guards off their posts.** Tip the police off about the steward on a door, and he's taken aside to the van for forty seconds.
- **Hired help.** Brendan flattens whoever you point at, and the stewards go after him instead of you.
- **Disguise.** The wardrobe card shows what an outfit opens and who sees through it.

**Gaps:**
- Consequences don't carry far enough. A bomb threat should make the turnstile searches harder for the rest of the day, and a traced phone should make your face known at the next match.
- Too few systems listen to each other:
  - the dealer's powder could spike the urn;
  - Brendan could be pointed at a steward guarding a door;
  - Mad Mick's bounty could become a running job board.

## 5. Acting and adapting: when it goes wrong, is it fun?

**Hitman:** A blown plan turns into a chase, a hide or a new disguise. Saves are cheap.

**Knockout Day:**
- **Heat ladder.** It goes from noticed, to caution, to evasion with a countdown, to alert, with a radar and a last-known-position ghost. That is close to MGSV.
- **Second chances.** A spotting steward gives you a beat of slow motion and a second and a half to stop his radio call.
- **Grassing witnesses.** This round's main addition, borrowed from RDR2 and Hitman. A fan who sees you, with no steward in earshot, says so and walks off to find one. While he goes, an eye with a draining ring sits over his head. You can:
  - pay him off;
  - lean on him (better with a weapon in hand);
  - flatten him before he gets there.

  Tribal loyalty cuts across it: a home fan watching you batter an away fan is more likely to cheer.
- **Combat.** It has a lunge, a sticky target and buffered input.

**Gap:** Getting arrested still ends the match. Hitman would hand you a checkpoint straight away. A "Back to the last checkpoint" button on the arrest card, sitting above the menu, would keep momentum.

## 6. Controls: is it playable on a phone?

**This round:**
- Tap the ground to walk there, with a ring marker that turns red if there's no way through. This follows Stardew Valley mobile's rules.
- Tap a person for the ring.
- A thumb resting on the stick no longer cancels a walk-over. It takes a deliberate push.
- Bribe only appears where money means something:
  - a grass ("Pay off");
  - a fan with a spare ticket ("Buy ticket");
  - stewards, opposition players and the referee.
- The phone is one button: pick the handset (yours or the burner), then a contact.

**Remaining:**
- The ring's labels are small. A long-press preview of what an action will do would help ("Pickpocket: 75% from behind").
- The More list duplicates the ring and could shrink to the few things that aren't about a person.

## 7. Tone

The comedy lands best when the systems produce it rather than the script. Examples:
- the stag do mistaking a fat bald man in a dress for Robbo;
- Shezleigh being hung up on mid-insult;
- someone shouting "Is that it?!" at a streaker.

More of that: every new system should have at least one way to go wrong that's funny.

## Priorities, in order

1. **Mission Stories for "fixes".** Three per ground, each a chain of overheard steps. This is the bridge from the goal to the sandbox.
2. **A deeper concourse and a big screen** as a landmark, plus trespass status shown on the floor.
3. **"Watch" in the ring** to track someone's routine for a minute.
4. **Consequences that carry over:** tougher searches after a bomb threat, and a traced phone flags your face at the next match.
5. **A quick checkpoint retry** on the arrest card.
